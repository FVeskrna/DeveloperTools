## What it does

Paste the raw text of a Dataverse plugin trace log — the Message Block plus Exception Details — and get a structured view: registration fields (depth, stage, mode, message, entity) extracted and annotated, timestamped trace lines turned into an execution timeline with per-step durations, and the exception chain unwound so the *innermost* message (the one that actually tells you what broke) is at the top instead of buried under three layers of `FaultException`. One click copies the whole summary as Markdown for a ticket or PR comment.

## Examples

**"The plugin failed but the error is useless."** The platform reports `Account credit limit exceeded` wrapped in a `FaultException`. Paste the trace: the parser surfaces the innermost `System.InvalidOperationException: Credit limit 50000 is below requested 125000` — the actual business rule that fired — and shows it was wrapped by the fault you saw in the UI.

**"Why is this update slow?"** A plugin that traces with timestamps gets a timeline. In the pre-filled sample, the step between *Retrieving parent account* and *Parent retrieved* shows `+674 ms` in red — one retrieve is eating most of the execution. Steps over 500 ms are flagged automatically.

**Attaching findings to a ticket.** COPY AS MARKDOWN produces a summary with the innermost exception, a field table, and the timeline — paste it straight into Azure DevOps or GitHub instead of a screenshot of the trace dialog.

## Edge cases & notes

- **Timestamps are whatever your plugin traced.** The parser recognizes `[HH:mm:ss.fff]` and ISO-style stamps at line starts; if your plugin doesn't trace times, you still get fields, steps, and the exception — just no timeline. (Adding `$"[{DateTime.UtcNow:HH:mm:ss.fff}]"` to your trace calls pays for itself the first slow plugin.)
- **Depth ≥ 2 is annotated** — if you're debugging an unexpected trigger, that's the first thing to check, before reading a single trace line.
- **Async plugins** (mode 1) log after the fact; their trace timestamps reflect the async service execution, not the user's save.
- **The 8-depth limit:** a plugin updating its own trigger entity loops until Dataverse aborts at depth 8 — the trace log for the *last* execution shows the depth counter, which is how you recognize the pattern.
- Exception stack lines (`   at …`) are kept in the full exception block but excluded from the summary — the innermost message plus the wrap chain is what belongs in a ticket title.
