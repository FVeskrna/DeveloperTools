## What it does

Convert an epoch timestamp to a readable date in any time zone, or parse a date string back into epoch seconds and milliseconds. A live ticker shows the current Unix time, and every conversion includes ISO 8601, your chosen zone with UTC offset, and a plain-English relative time ("5 months ago").

## Examples

**Reading a database timestamp.** `1735689600` converts to `2025-01-01T00:00:00.000Z` — and to `Wed, 01 Jan 2025, 01:00:00 GMT+1` if you pick `Europe/Prague`. The relative line tells you at a glance it's over a year in the past.

**A JavaScript timestamp from `Date.now()`.** `1751804000000` is 13 digits, so auto-detect treats it as milliseconds — no need to trim three zeros before pasting.

**Scheduling a config value.** Type `2026-12-31T23:59:00Z` in the date field to get `1798761540` seconds for a cron-adjacent config or a JWT `exp` claim.

## Edge cases & notes

- **Time zone ≠ offset.** `Europe/Prague` is +01:00 in winter and +02:00 in summer; the converter applies the correct historical DST rule for the specific instant, which a fixed offset cannot do.
- **Date-only strings** like `2026-07-06` are interpreted as midnight **UTC** per the ISO 8601 spec — not local midnight. Add an explicit time and offset to avoid ambiguity.
- **Leap seconds** do not exist in Unix time; it counts 86,400 seconds per day, always. The 27 leap seconds since 1972 are invisible here, as they are to your database.
- The parser accepts ISO 8601 and RFC 2822 (`Mon, 06 Jul 2026 12:00:00 GMT`); ambiguous regional formats like `07/06/2026` are best avoided.
