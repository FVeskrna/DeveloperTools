## What it does

Paste a cron expression and get it back in plain English, along with the next five times it will fire in a timezone of your choice and a table showing exactly which values each of the five fields matches. Presets cover the schedules everyone reaches for; 6-field (Quartz/seconds) input is detected and explained rather than misparsed.

## Examples

**`*/15 * * * *`** — the pre-filled sample — reads as *Every 15 minutes*, with the field table showing minutes 0, 15, 30, 45 and every value elsewhere.

**`0 9 * * 1-5`** (the WEEKDAYS 9:00 preset) reads as *At 09:00 on Monday through Friday*. Switch the timezone selector between UTC and your local zone and watch the run times shift — the classic "why did my report arrive at 7 a.m.?" bug, reproduced safely.

**`0 0 1 1 *`** reads as *At 00:00 on day 1 of the month in January* — a yearly job. The next-runs list shows five consecutive New Year midnights, which confirms the intent faster than any manual reading of the fields.

## Edge cases & notes

- **Day fields OR together.** When both day-of-month and day-of-week are restricted, matching either fires the job (see FAQ) — the single most common cron misreading.
- **`0` and `7` both mean Sunday** in the day-of-week field; the parser normalizes them.
- **Day 31 in a 30-day month** simply never matches — the preview skips those months, which is how you catch a "monthly" job that silently misses September.
- **DST:** a job scheduled inside the spring-forward gap (e.g. 02:30 on transition night in Europe) has no real wall-clock moment; real cron daemons differ in what they do, and the preview here resolves it to the nearest valid instant. Avoid scheduling inside 02:00–03:00 local if this matters to you.
- Steps apply to ranges: `10-30/5` = 10, 15, 20, 25, 30; a bare `5/15` (Vixie extension) means "from 5 to max, step 15".
